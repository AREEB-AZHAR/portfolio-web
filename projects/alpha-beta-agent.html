01 // THE STARTING POINT

The combinatorial explosion problem.
Brute force search fails exponentially.

In zero-sum discrete board games, the branching factor grows exponentially with search depth. A standard Minimax search attempting to look 6 to 8 moves ahead requires evaluating millions of board configurations, causing unacceptable delays in real-time play.

By implementing Alpha-Beta pruning, the search tree can be aggressively cut whenever a candidate move is proven strictly inferior to an already explored branch, reducing time complexity from O(bd) down toward O(bd/2) in optimal move orderings.

STEP 01

Alpha & Beta Bound Narrowing

The algorithm maintains two bounds: $\alpha$ (the minimum score the maximizing player is assured of) and $\beta$ (the maximum score the minimizing player is assured of). The instant $\beta \le \alpha$, subsequent children of the current node are pruned immediately.

STEP 02

Transposition Hash Table

Identical game states can be reached through different permutations of moves (transpositions). An in-memory Zobrist-hashing transposition cache records evaluated state scores, eliminating re-evaluation of previously traversed sub-trees.

STEP 03

Positional Evaluation Matrices

Rather than merely counting pieces, the heuristic evaluation function computes territory control, corner leverage, mobility advantage, and parity, allowing the AI to sacrifice short-term material for decisive strategic endgames.
